# Discussion Summary 1
**Project:** Digital Privacy Agreement Audit  
**Team Members:** Tirth Laheri, Jay Beladiya

## Objective
The purpose of this discussion was to understand the project requirements, decide how to divide the workload, and identify a strategy for comparing privacy agreements from different organizations.

## Discussion

We began by reviewing the project instructions together and discussing the expected deliverables, including the individual privacy policy analyses, regulatory comparison, summary matrices, and final report. Since the project required analyzing organizations from different industries, we agreed that selecting companies with diverse business models would provide a more meaningful comparison of privacy practices.

Before deciding on the final companies, both team members reviewed privacy policies from several organizations to understand the differences in their privacy practices. **Tirth reviewed Google, HoYoverse (Genshin Impact), FedEx, Discord, Robinhood, Riot Games, USPS, Amazon, PayPal, Discover, Meta, Netflix, Ubisoft, and Microsoft.** After comparing their privacy practices, industry representation, and regulatory coverage, **Tirth selected Robinhood, Riot Games, and USPS** for the detailed analysis because they represented the financial services, gaming, and government sectors.

Similarly, **Jay reviewed Amazon, PayPal, Discover, Meta, Netflix, Ubisoft, Google, Microsoft, Robinhood, Riot Games, USPS, Discord, FedEx, and HoYoverse (Genshin Impact).** After evaluating the available options, **Jay selected Amazon, PayPal, Discover, Meta, Netflix, Ubisoft, Google, and Microsoft** for detailed analysis. These companies were chosen to provide strong representation across e-commerce, finance, social media, entertainment, gaming, and technology.

During the discussion, we identified an important challenge. Each company structures its privacy policy differently, making direct comparisons difficult. Some organizations organize their policies by products or regions, while others focus on user rights or legal compliance. We realized that comparing the policies without a common evaluation framework could lead to inconsistent findings and make it difficult to combine our analyses into a single report.

To address this issue, we agreed to use a standardized comparison framework. Every privacy policy would be evaluated using the same criteria, including the types of data collected, methods of collection, data sharing practices, user rights, security measures, opt-out options, policy updates, and compliance with major privacy regulations such as the GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and VCDPA.

## Outcome

- Reviewed the assignment requirements and project deliverables.
- Both team members reviewed multiple privacy policies before selecting the final companies for detailed analysis.
- Tirth selected Robinhood, Riot Games, and USPS, while Jay selected Amazon, PayPal, Discover, Meta, Netflix, Ubisoft, Google, and Microsoft.
- Identified differences in privacy policy structures as the primary challenge.
- Agreed to use a standardized comparison framework for all organizations.
- Planned a second discussion after completing the individual analyses to combine findings into the final report.
